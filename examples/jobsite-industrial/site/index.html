Questions

Common questions

Don't see yours? Call (817) 555-0188 and ask.

How much does an electrician cost?

Small jobs like replacing an outlet or switch often run $150 to $350, depending on how many we do in one visit. Larger work varies more: a 200-amp panel upgrade commonly runs $1,500 to $4,000, and a Level 2 EV charger install $800 to $2,500. We give you a flat price before we start.

Do I need a permit?

New circuits, panel upgrades, EV chargers, generators and rewiring almost always need a permit. Swapping a fixture or outlet usually doesn't. We pull the permit when it's required and include it in the price, and the inspection protects you when you sell the house.

Why does my breaker keep tripping?

Usually the circuit is overloaded, something plugged into it has a fault, or there's a short or loose connection. A breaker that trips is doing its job, so don't keep resetting it or swap in a bigger one. We'll find the cause.

Is my electrical panel safe?

Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) and Zinsco panels have a history of breakers that may not trip when they should, and many electricians recommend replacing them. Warning signs in any panel include rust, scorch marks, buzzing or breakers that feel hot. We can inspect it and tell you what we see.

How do I know if my wiring is outdated?

Warning signs include two-prong outlets throughout the house, fuses instead of breakers, lights that dim when appliances start, warm outlets or switch plates and wiring with cloth insulation. Homes built before the 1960s are worth having checked, especially before adding big loads like an EV charger or heat pump.
